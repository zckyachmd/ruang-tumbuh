# WHO growth comparison: validation and reuse gates

**Status:** research note. Ruang Tumbuh does not currently calculate or display WHO comparisons. Keep this feature disabled until the gates below are met.

**Intended use:** noncommercial, as confirmed by the user on 2026-11-02. Noncommercial status does not by itself establish a right to redistribute WHO data or prove that this family-record app falls within the dataset terms' public-health purpose.

## What WHO sources establish

- WHO Child Growth Standards charts for boys and girls cover birth through five years (60 completed months). The chart set includes length/height-for-age, weight-for-age, weight-for-length/height, and BMI-for-age. WHO describes its training course as intended primarily for health providers who measure and assess child growth, and it includes equipment calibration and measurement procedures. [WHO Child Growth Standards](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards)
- WHO's 2025 FAQ says the 2006 standards apply regardless of ethnicity, socioeconomic status, and feeding, and distinguishes the separate 2007 reference for ages 5–19. Ruang Tumbuh must not extend the under-five standard past its eligible age or silently substitute the 2007 reference. [WHO Child Growth Standards Q&A](https://www.who.int/news-room/questions-and-answers/item/child-growth-standards)
- The standards are sex-specific. WHO's survey guide states z-scores are not calculated without sex; missing weight or length/height prevents the corresponding measure-related indices. [WHO Anthro Survey Analyser quick guide](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/software/anthro-survey-analyser-quickguide.pdf?sfvrsn=dc7ddc6f_6)
- For length/height indicators, WHO recommends recumbent length before age 24 months and standing height from age 24 months. If the recorded position is opposite the age convention, WHO Anthro's under-five methodology adds 0.7 cm to standing height below 24 months, or subtracts 0.7 cm from recumbent length from 24 months. Measurement position must therefore be recorded; the conversion does not validate how the measurement was taken. [WHO Anthro manual](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/software/anthro-pc-manual-v322.pdf), [WHO Survey Analyser quick guide](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/software/anthro-survey-analyser-quickguide.pdf?sfvrsn=dc7ddc6f_6)
- WHO's tool page distinguishes individual assessment from survey analysis. The legacy WHO Anthro tool includes individual assessment and calculates individual z-scores; the newer Survey Analyser targets survey analysis and changes prevalence-estimate handling. Its under-five prevalence cutoff is below 60 months, while legacy Anthro's survey estimates include through 60.99 months. These survey cutoffs must not be treated as interchangeable individual-chart eligibility rules. [WHO Anthro tools and software](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards/software)
- WHO's expanded-table instructions say length/height-for-age has a discontinuity between day 730 and 731 because the measurement switches from recumbent length to standing height at age two. Age-based expanded tables use days and extend to day 1856 (60.98 months) so that all children through 60 completed months are covered. For chart production, WHO gives 30.4375 days per month and recommends checking reproduced curves against the WHO tables. Implement age and the length/height boundary from dates in days; do not round age to whole months. [WHO expanded-table instructions](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/indicators/instructions-en.pdf?sfvrsn=5cec8c61_23)
- WHO links to the R package `anthro` and describes it as reflecting methodology recommended in the joint WHO/UNICEF guide. WHO also says the newer Survey Analyser added validation for measurement position. The WHO-maintained `anthro` source accepts exact age in days and explicit `L`/`H` position, applies the age-two 0.7 cm conversion, and only calculates z-scores below 60 months. It imputes missing measurement position from age (or length when age is also missing), so Ruang Tumbuh should reject missing position rather than inherit that convenience. Its current source metadata says GPL-3; that package licence does not by itself establish rights for separate WHO tables or compatibility with this app's distribution. [WHO Anthro tools and software](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards/software), [WHO anthro source](https://github.com/WorldHealthOrganization/anthro/blob/master/R/z-score.R), [package licence metadata](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/WorldHealthOrganization/anthro/master/DESCRIPTION)
- WHO's Survey Analyser quick guide defines implausible-value flags for calculated z-scores: weight-for-age below -6 or above +5; length/height-for-age below -6 or above +6; weight-for-length/height below -5 or above +5; and BMI-for-age below -5 or above +5. These are flags on derived indicators, not validated bounds for raw measurements, diagnoses, or proof that a measurement is false. The survey workflow excludes a flagged index from prevalence/statistic calculations while retaining all measurements. Its flag table does not define a head-circumference flag. For this family-record app, retain the entered measurement, mark any applicable flagged comparison for review, and do not present it as an unqualified WHO assessment; do not invent a head-circumference cutoff. [WHO Survey Analyser quick guide, Table 5 and Appendix](https://www.who.int/docs/default-source/child-growth/child-growth-standards/software/anthro-survey-analyser-quickguide.pdf?sfvrsn=dc7ddc6f_6), [WHO software overview](https://www.who.int/toolkits/child-growth-standards/software)

## Reuse and distribution gate

WHO says its publications have been issued under CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO since 12 November 2016; publications from before 2017 are not reissued under that licence. Its copyright page says reuse of materials outside that licence requires permission, including tables and technical information in electronic database products; commercial use requires permission. WHO also cautions that publications may contain third-party material with separate rights. [WHO Open Access policy](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/open-access), [WHO copyright and licensing](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright)

WHO's dataset terms define machine-readable data collections and data derived from them (including tables and charts), and grant use, reproduction, distribution and inclusion in other products for public-health purposes. They require attribution and third-party clearance, and prohibit use in or with promotion of a commercial enterprise, product or service; changes beyond minimal adaptation require prior written authorization. This language is relevant to the downloadable growth data, but the policy does not name the growth-standard workbooks individually. [WHO dataset terms](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/data-policy/terms-and-conditions)

The current [WHO length/height-for-age page](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards/standards/length-height-for-age) lists sex-specific z-score tables for birth–2 years and 2–5 years, plus expanded z-score workbooks. The inspected workbook properties and visible table page did not state an asset-specific licence. WHO's copyright page separately says permission is required for WHO materials outside its CC BY-NC-SA 3.0 IGO licence, including tables and technical information in electronic database products, and for commercial use. Because the applicable terms for the exact downloadable files and this product's use context are not established, obtain written clarification from WHO for the selected files and intended distribution before bundling them. This is a clearance gap, not a conclusion that use is prohibited. [WHO copyright and licensing](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/copyright)

The WHO Child Growth Standards date to 2006, so the general post-2016 publication licence cannot be assumed for the original tables or downloadable data files. Before bundling or distributing a table, chart, digitized curve, derived reference dataset, or WHO software component, record the exact source asset, its own copyright/license statement and provenance; obtain written permission where required. The dataset terms' public-health purpose and redistribution conditions still need to be matched to this noncommercial family-record use. This is a conservative reading of WHO's published policies, not legal advice.

WHO's under-five reference pages list sex-specific charts and downloadable tables for [weight-for-age](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards/standards/weight-for-age), [weight-for-length/height](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards/standards/weight-for-length-height), [length/height-for-age](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards/standards/length-height-for-age), and [head circumference-for-age](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards/standards/head-circumference-for-age); WHO also lists BMI-for-age in its [standards overview](https://www.who.int/tools/child-growth-standards). This is a source inventory, not approval to bundle the files or a decision about which indicators to implement. Exact asset versions and rights remain to be pinned.

WHO's permissions page says permission is not required for material released under a Creative Commons licence when reuse is noncommercial and outside a commercial context. For other WHO-copyrighted material, WHO asks for a detailed request with a direct link to the material; a successful submission returns a reference ID. The page also directs requesters to contact the credited publisher when the material is third-party. This does not identify the licence on the 2006 growth workbooks, so it supplies the route for clarification rather than clearing those files. [WHO permissions request](https://www.who.int/about/policies/publishing/permissions)

Before requesting clearance, select the indicators and exact downloads to include, then submit their direct WHO asset links and describe this as a noncommercial family record app. Ask WHO whether each workbook is covered by the dataset terms or another licence, whether the stated use is within “public health purposes,” and whether bundling the unmodified tables in a self-hosted application is allowed. Keep the returned reference ID and written response with the asset inventory; do not bundle the tables while the answer is pending.

Ready-to-fill request text and the direct URLs for the ten inspected workbooks are in [WHO-permission-request-draft.md](WHO-permission-request-draft.md). Confirm the deployment/distribution detail before submitting.

## Acceptance gates before enabling a comparison

1. **Rights:** inventory each exact chart/table/data/software asset, record source URL, version/date, owner, licence and any third-party attribution. Confirm the licence permits the actual app distribution and use context; obtain permission if needed. Do not bundle assets while this is unresolved.
2. **Method:** choose and name the intended indicator(s) and the valid age/range for each. Specify sex, date-of-birth/measurement-date handling, units, and position rules. Keep length and height as distinct recorded facts; do not infer position silently.
3. **Reference validation:** pin a WHO method/version and reference dataset checksum. Compare output against independently sourced WHO Anthro/`anthro` fixtures at boundary ages, both sexes, indicator-specific limits, and position-conversion cases. Reject missing age, sex, or measurement position; do not use `anthro`'s position imputation. Keep raw measurements even when a derived indicator is unavailable or flagged. For a WHO-flagged index, show a review flag and withhold an unqualified comparison; do not delete the measurement or treat survey-analysis exclusion as clinical guidance. Do not implement head-circumference flags without a pinned WHO rule and independent fixtures.
4. **Display:** compare only compatible measurements and show the measurement date, indicator, units, source/version and missing-data state. Do not join measurements from different dates into one index silently. Explain that a plotted comparison is not a diagnosis and does not replace assessment by a qualified health professional.
5. **Release:** retain the fixtures, derivation notes, rights evidence and review record with the implementation. Keep the feature disabled until all gates pass.

## Current project boundary

Ruang Tumbuh records weight, length, height and head circumference. Length now requires a recorded `recumbent` position and height `standing` in the API and chat; the Mini App exposes that position explicitly. Tool/method details are optional and only retained when supplied. These checks validate capture structure, not measurement technique or clinical plausibility: any positive value can still be stored, and no WHO reference range or z-score is checked. The UI states that WHO comparison is unavailable and the measurement is not a diagnosis; the PRD requires source/version/licence, indicator units/ranges, independent fixtures, distinct length and height, explicit missing coverage, and no cross-date joins. No WHO dataset or calculation is active. [Project PRD](../PRD.md)
